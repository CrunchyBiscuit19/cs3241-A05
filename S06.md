# S06 — Illumination and Shading

## Illumination and Shading
* Illumination Model — Given a surface point, light sources and the viewer, compute the colour of that point.
* Shading — Determine the colour of a polygon.
* Input is a polygonal mesh with its topology, so each polygon's neighbours are known.

## Phong Illumination Equation (PIE)
* Phong model — Local point reflection model, a compromise between image quality and speed.
* ![[Pasted image 20260925130951.webp|741]]
* $I_{\text{Phong}} = \underbrace{I_a K_a}_{\text{ambient}} + \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\text{no. lights}} \left( \underbrace{f_{\text{att},i} I_{p,i} K_d (N \cdot L_i)}_{\text{diffuse}} + \underbrace{f_{\text{att},i} I_{p,i} K_s \max(0, R_i \cdot V)^n}_{\text{specular}} \right)$
* Capital letters are RGB vectors, lower case are constants.
* $I_a K_a$ etc. are component-wise products. Only $N \cdot L$ and $R \cdot V$ are dot products.
* Normalize every direction vector.

### Material Properties
* Ambient $K_a$, diffuse $K_{d}$, specular $K_s$ coefficients. Each a 3-colour vector with values in $[0,1]$.
* Shininess $n \in [1,500\text{-ish}]$ .
### Ambient Term
* Uniform lighting on every point of every surface, luminance $I_a = [i_{ar}\ i_{ag}\ i_{ab}]^T$.
* Every surface receives the same ambient colour and intensity. Not view dependent.
* $I_a K_a = [i_{ar}k_{ar}\ \ i_{ag}k_{ag}\ \ i_{ab}k_{ab}]^T$.
### Diffuse Term
* Different colors to different points on the surface according to the light positions and surface normals.
* $I_{p}$ specifices color / intensity of conventional light source, and $f_{\text{att}}$ is the attenuation factor of the incoming light.
* $f_{\text{att}} I_p = (a + bd + cd^2)^{-1} I_p$ where the $a,b,c$ are user-defined constants.
* $f_{\text{att}} I_p K_d (N \cdot L)$
#### Lambert's Cosine Law
* $\propto \cos\theta = N \cdot L$ where $L$ is unit vector from the surface point to the light.
* Beam of cross-section $dA$ at incidence $\theta$ spreads over $\dfrac{dA}{\cos\theta}$, so energy received $\propto \cos\theta$.
* ![[Pasted image 20260925132211.webp|126]]
* ![[Pasted image 20260925131922.webp|390]]
### Specular Term
* Highlights for shiny objects. View dependent, the highlight moves as the viewer moves.
* Shininess is inversely proportional to the size of the highlight.
* $\propto = \cos^{-1}(R \cdot V)$, $R = 2(N \cdot L)N - L$.
* $f_{\text{att}} I_p K_s \max(0, R \cdot V)^n$ where $R$ is reflection vector, $V$ is unit vector from the surface point 
  to the viewer. $\alpha = \cos^{-1}(R \cdot V)$, and $n$ is shininess coefficient. 
* Larger $n$ gives a smaller, sharper highlight.
* ![[Pasted image 20260925132929.webp|254]]

#### Reflection Vector Derivation
* $N' = (N \cdot L)N$, since $|N'| = N \cdot L$
* $S = N' - L$
* $R = L + 2S = 2N' - L = 2(N \cdot L)N - L$
* ![[Pasted image 20260925133701.webp|265]]

## Light Sources

### Light Types

#### Point Lights
* Light at position $p$, emitting in every direction with colour / intensity $I_p$.
* Received light weakens with distance, see Attenuation.
* $L$ points from each surface point to $p$, so it differs across the surface.

#### Spot Lights
* Point light $p$ restricted to a cone of angle $2\theta$, where $\theta$ is cutoff angle, $v_s$ is spotlight direction, and $s$ is vector from $p$ to a surface point.
* A surface point is lit if $\phi \le \theta$, where $\phi = \cos^{-1}(s \cdot v_s)$ with $s$, $v_s$ unit vectors.
* Soft cutoff where intensity falls off towards the cone edge. $I_p' = I_p (\cos\phi)^n = I_p (s \cdot v_s)^n$, where $n$ is the spotlight exponent.
* ![[Pasted image 20260925140100.webp|169]] ![[Pasted image 20260925140129.webp|390]]

#### Parallel / Directional Lights
* Light too far away (eg. the sun), every beam has the same direction.
* $L$ is the same for every surface point.

### Attenuation
* Light intensities get weaker with distance $d$.
* Weakness represented by attenuation factor $f_{\text{att}} = (a + bd + cd^2)^{-1}$ with user-defined constants $a$, $b$, $c$.
### Surface Normal
* For points $A, B, C$, $N = (B - A) \times (C - A) = D \times E = (D_{2}E_{3} - D_{3}E_{2},\ D_{3}E_{1} - D_{1}E_{3},\ D_{1}E_{2} - D_{2}E_{1})$.
* Take a vertex $Q$ not on the face. If $(Q - A) \cdot N < 0$, $N$ points outward, keep it. If $> 0$, flip to $-N$.
  eg. $a = (0,0,0)$, $b = (0,0,9)$, $c = (0,9,0)$, $d = (9,0,0)$. Face $adb$ with $N = (0,-81,0)$ gives $(c - a) \cdot N = -729 < 0$, keep.
* If the vertex order is given, the order defines $N$. Only use the check when choosing the order yourself.
* For a curved surface, normal is perpendicular to the tangent plane at the point.

## Shading

### Flat `glShadeModel(GL_FLAT)`
* Colour computed at 1 point per polygon (centre or a corner).
* Use normals of each polygon.
* Distinct colour differences between neighbouring polygons.
* ![[Pasted image 20260925153807.webp|189]]

### Gouraud `glShadeModel(GL_SMOOTH)`
* Colours computed for each vertex, then colours interpolated in SCA.
* Use vertex normals, the average of the normals of the polygons sharing that vertex.
* (The effect of doing PIE on the vertex shader, LearnOpenGL)
* ![[Pasted image 20260925154327.webp|223]] ![[Pasted image 20260925154633.webp|151]]

### Phong (Not in Ancient OpenGL)
* Colours computed for each pixel.
* Vertex normals interpolated for each pixel in SCA, then evaluate PIE there. 
* Position and normal must be in the same coordinate system as the lights and viewer (transform back to world coordinates).
* Phong reproduces highlights more faithfully, Gouraud only linearly interpolates colours and may miss a highlight.
* ![[Pasted image 20260925154546.webp|188]]


## Global vs Non-global Illumination
|               | Global / Ray Tracing            | Non-Global / Classic Rasterization                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Considers     | All physical light interactions | Only light source, surface point and viewer directly |
| Other objects | Included                        | Ignored                                              |
| Result        | Photorealistic, slow            | Less realistic, fast                                 |
| Use           | Offline Rendering               | Real-time Rendering                                  |

## Problems with Interpolated Shading
* Non-global effects — No shadows.
* Polygonal silhouette — The outline stays polygonal however smooth the shading.
* Orientation dependence — The interpolated result changes when the polygon rotates.
* Shared vertices — Vertex on a neighbouring polygon's edge gets inconsistent values.
* Misleading vertex normals — Averaged normals can hide the real shape.

## Other Shading
* Toon shading (cel-shading) — Round $(N \cdot L)$ and $(R \cdot V)^n$ to steps. eg. 0 or 1.
* ![[Pasted image 20260925160428.webp|345]]
* Bump mapping — Like texture mapping, but maps a distortion of the normals instead of colours.
